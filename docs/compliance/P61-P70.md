## 1. Model: Configuration-as-Compliance

The SAVANT FRAMEWORK does not fork its architecture per jurisdiction. It **configures** compliance. The core systems — [CLAI-OS](../clai-os/deterministic-engine.md), the [FHIR R4 layer](../clai-os/fhir-r4-native.md), [AEGIS-NG](../aegis-ng/node-types.md) — are jurisdiction-neutral invariants. Each regional adapter, P61 through P70, is a versioned configuration bundle that binds the invariant core to a jurisdiction's regulatory anchors: data-residency settings, consent flows, retention clocks, identity-resolution rules, reporting formats, and audit-disclosure interfaces.

Three properties define the model:

1. **The core never changes.** A compliance requirement that cannot be met by configuration exposes a defect in the core's neutrality, and is escalated as such — it is never patched into the core under the adapter's name.
2. **Adapters are open specifications.** The ten adapter specifications in this document are open, like the FHIR layer: any sovereign partner may implement, inspect, or extend them. Compliance that cannot be inspected is a claim, not a control.
3. **Adapters are governed artifacts.** Each adapter version passes an S50 class-C checkpoint, and its predicate bundle includes a regression battery: a new adapter version may tighten obligations, never silently loosen them.

## 3. Anatomy of an Adapter

Every adapter is the same six-part configuration bundle; the openness of the specification means the anatomy is fully stated here and applies uniformly to P61–P70:

| Part | Content | Example (P61) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Residency map | Where ciphertext and key material may rest | In-country hub storage; no extra-jurisdiction replicas |
| 2. Consent binding | How consent events map to Delegation Key issuance/revocation ([FHIR layer §3](../clai-os/fhir-r4-native.md)) | NDPR-compliant consent record signed per issuance |
| 3. Identity rules | Resolution rules between pseudonymous system ID and legal identity | Ward/LGA-level location only at rest; legal identity under patient-held mapping |
| 4. Retention & erasure clocks | Retention periods; crypto-shredding invocation rules | Erasure by REK destruction; certificate of crypto-shredding |
| 5. Reporting interfaces | Regulator-facing disclosure formats and breach-notification clocks | NITDA-format incident disclosure, 72 h clock |
| 6. Radio/spectrum annex | Type-approval and ISM-band conditions for AEGIS-NG | NCC type-approval records per node type, 433 MHz conditions |

## 4. The Jurisdiction Compiler — PPA-006 (Planned, Not Filed)

A jurisdiction outside the 24 above currently requires a hand-authored adapter following the §3 anatomy. The **Jurisdiction Compiler** — a tool that generates a draft adapter bundle from a structured regulatory questionnaire, for human legal review before ratification — is a planned component identified as **PPA-006**. Its status is disclosed exactly: **planned, not filed.** It is not part of any current deliverable, it appears in no budget as an existing capability, and no grant narrative may cite it except as a named future work item. This disclosure is itself a compliance control: the difference between a roadmap and a misrepresentation is whether the label travels with the item.

## 5. Verification and Audit Posture

Because adapters are configuration, their verification is mechanical: for each adapter, the predicate bundle checks (a) residency settings against the residency map, (b) consent flows against the binding rules, (c) retention clocks against the erasure spec, and (d) reporting formats against a regulator-format fixture. A deployed system can therefore demonstrate its compliance posture to an auditor by *showing its configuration and its predicate results* — not by producing policy prose. Regulators receive, on request: the adapter version, its S50 verdict record, and the live predicate battery output. This is what "configuration-as-compliance" buys an operator: compliance that is demonstrable on demand rather than arguable on retainer.

## Falsifiability Test

**Claim:** Ten adapters bind 24 jurisdictions by configuration alone, with the core unchanged and every posture mechanically verifiable.

**Test 1 (count integrity):** Sum the jurisdiction column of §2. Any total other than 24, or any jurisdiction listed under two adapters, falsifies the registry; record a REGRESSION record (S50 class C).

**Test 2 (configuration purity):** For any adapter, diff the deployed core build against the jurisdiction-neutral reference build. Any code-level divergence (as opposed to configuration-bundle divergence) falsifies §1 property 1 and is escalated as a core-neutrality defect.

**Test 3 (PPA-006 honesty):** Grep all submitted grant narratives and budget documents for "Jurisdiction Compiler." Any occurrence not labeled planned/not-filed falsifies §4 and is a class-A defect against the citing document.
