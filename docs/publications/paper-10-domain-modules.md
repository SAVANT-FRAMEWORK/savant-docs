## Abstract

Domain instantiations of the constitutional runtime in clinical human-good applications: pediatric guardianship, maternal protection, palliative navigation, emergency architecture, and antimicrobial-resistance response — each as a governed module over the formal kernel.

## Merit

A formal kernel earns its keep by instantiating. This paper is the demonstration: five clinical human-good modules — pediatric guardianship, maternal protection, palliative navigation, emergency architecture, and antimicrobial-resistance response — each built as a governed module over the shared kernel rather than as a bespoke artifact.

For the practitioner and the procurement reviewer, the merit is concreteness: each module shows what the authority algebra, the coupling law, and the objective lattice look like when they meet a real clinical domain, including the places where the domain pushes back. For the researcher, the modules are the corpus's answer to the charge of abstraction — the same kernel, five different clinical moral geometries, no special pleading. The antimicrobial-resistance module in particular shows the framework handling a collective-action problem, not only individual-care decisions.
