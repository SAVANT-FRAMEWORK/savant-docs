## Abstract

The constraint structure over money, time, workforce, infrastructure, supply chain and law; the monotone feasibility operator and its fixed-point admissible set; feasible optimality; feasibility certificates and the price-of-infeasibility certificate as first-class constitutional objects.

## Merit

Most clinical AI is specified against an implicit resource assumption that fails in most of the world. This paper puts the constraint structure on the table — money, time, workforce, infrastructure, supply chain, and law — and builds the mathematics of deciding inside it: a monotone feasibility operator whose fixed point is the admissible set, feasible optimality as the design target, and feasibility certificates as first-class constitutional objects.

The merit for global-health planners and policymakers is the price-of-infeasibility certificate: when the admissible set is empty, the system does not silently fail or silently proceed — it issues a priced, auditable statement of what the constraint cost. Infeasibility becomes reportable. For the engineer, the monotone operator gives a computable fixed-point construction rather than a heuristic relaxation, and the falsification conditions say exactly what would break the claim.
