## Merit

The blueprints turn the constitutional machinery outward, toward institutions. This first one proposes a conserved-quantity science of institutional trust: from the DPT impossibility theorem to metered governance, capture early warning, and the legitimacy-adjusted cost of capital. Its instruments — the Legitimacy Accounting Standard, the trilemma stated as procurement law, a sovereign-scale capture early-warning instrument, refusal-set economics, and zero-knowledge attestation of authority — are stated as a pre-registered empirical programme with explicit kill criteria.

For the governance scholar, the merit is that legitimacy is treated as something that can be metered, conserved, and spent — with an accounting standard attached — rather than as a sentiment polled after the fact. For the policy institution, capture early warning is specified as an instrument with hypotheses and kill criteria, not as a metaphor. The honest boundary is stated in the document itself: this public edition omits the internal commissioning, vehicle, intellectual-property, and funding apparatus of the original blueprint; the instrument, its hypotheses, and its kill criteria are complete.
