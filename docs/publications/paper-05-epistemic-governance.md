## Abstract

Evidence as a typed object with a half-life; source hierarchy and chain of epistemic custody; contradiction as suspension rather than averaging; calibration as a measured constitutional property; and the coupling law making executable authority a monotone function of evidence quality.

## Merit

This paper closes the loop between what a system knows and what it is allowed to do. Evidence is a typed object with a half-life; sources sit in a hierarchy with a chain of epistemic custody; contradiction triggers suspension rather than averaging; calibration is a measured constitutional property; and the coupling law makes executable authority a monotone function of evidence quality.

For the engineer, the coupling law is the runtime rule that connects data quality to permitted action — as evidence decays or contradicts, authority contracts through the machinery of Paper 1 rather than through an operator's discretion. For the researcher, treating contradiction as suspension rather than averaging is a clean break with score-blending practice and is stated precisely enough to be argued with. For the policymaker, the paper is a formal statement of why provenance and dataset shift are governance events, not data-engineering details.
