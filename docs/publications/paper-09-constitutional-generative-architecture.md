## Abstract

The generative layer: how governed systems produce new protocols under constitutional constraint, the gated automaton lifecycle, and why self-ablation — the disciplined retirement of generated protocols — is formally harder than generation.

## Merit

A governed system that cannot extend itself will be routed around; one that extends itself without discipline becomes ungovernable. This paper specifies the generative layer: how new protocols are produced under constitutional constraint, the gated automaton lifecycle every generated protocol must pass through, and the paper's central structural result — self-ablation, the disciplined retirement of generated protocols, is formally harder than generation.

For the researcher in self-modifying systems, that asymmetry is the contribution: birth and death of rules are treated as governed transitions with different proof burdens, and the paper states why retirement is the harder direction rather than assuming symmetry. For the engineer, the gated lifecycle is directly implementable — generation produces candidates, not law, and each gate is a named checkpoint with preconditions. For the governance scholar, the paper addresses the question every living legal system faces: how a constitution may grow without ceasing to be one.
