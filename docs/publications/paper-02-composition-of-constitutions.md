## Abstract

The composition algebra that converts a collection of protocols into a governed system: constraint objects, four composition operators, partial-order precedence with human judgment as lattice completion, irreversibility and temporal weighting, and the adjudication economy.

## Merit

A single protocol is easy to govern; the operative condition of any real deployment is several constitutional sources producing conflicting obligations at once. This paper supplies the algebra for that condition: constraint objects, four composition operators, and a partial-order precedence relation in which human judgment enters not as an escape hatch but as the lattice completion — the formally designated resolver of what the order leaves incomparable.

For the governance scholar, the adjudication economy is the distinctive move: conflict resolution is treated as a resource with costs and incentives, not as an error path. For the engineer, the composition operators are directly implementable and give a precise meaning to "these two rule sets disagree." For the policymaker, the irreversibility and temporal-weighting machinery captures something statute drafters know but formal frameworks rarely say: some decisions cannot be walked back, and recent authority is not automatically senior authority.

## Guidance for Use

**Who should read this work**

- Engineers composing multiple policy or rule systems into one governed deployment.
- Governance scholars interested in adjudication formalized rather than asserted.

- A compositional semantics: a collection of protocols becomes a single governed system with defined conflict behavior.
- A principled seat for human judgment — as lattice completion, with its invocation logged and costed.
- The precedence machinery assumed by the domain modules of Paper 10 and the runtime of Paper 12.
