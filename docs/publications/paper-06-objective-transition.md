## Abstract

The objective-state lattice (curative, life-prolonging, palliative, comfort), authorized transitions with asymmetric thresholds, the obsolete-objective invariant, goals-of-care architecture, and the death-to-learning pipeline that returns terminal cases to the registry as knowledge.

## Merit

Clinical systems drift in their objectives long before they drift in their outputs, and most governance frameworks have no name for that failure. This paper names it. The objective-state lattice — curative, life-prolonging, palliative, comfort — with authorized transitions gated by asymmetric thresholds, the obsolete-objective invariant that forbids silent pursuit of a retired goal, the goals-of-care architecture, and the death-to-learning pipeline that returns terminal cases to the registry as knowledge.

For the clinical informaticist, goals of care become executable state transitions rather than free-text notes, with escalation and de-escalation deliberately priced differently. For the health policymaker, the obsolete-objective invariant is a regulatory-grade requirement: a system continuing to optimize an abandoned objective is a defined violation, not a subtlety. The asymmetric thresholds encode a clinical intuition formally — moving toward comfort should be easier than moving away from cure — and the paper says so as a proof obligation, not a sentiment.
