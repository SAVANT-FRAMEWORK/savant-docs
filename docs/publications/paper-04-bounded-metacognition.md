## Abstract

Recursive self-audit made operational: the audit stack, decision provenance in place of reasoning-trace disclosure, uncertainty as a typed constitutional object, bias detection as a monitored absence, stopping rules as executable predicates, and the diminishing-returns calculus of recursion depth.

## Merit

Self-audit is usually invoked as a virtue and left unbounded; this paper makes it operational and then asks the harder question of when to stop. The audit stack, decision provenance in place of reasoning-trace disclosure, uncertainty as a typed constitutional object, bias detection as a monitored absence, stopping rules as executable predicates, and a diminishing-returns calculus over recursion depth.

Its merit is unusually concrete, because its central claim has been executed. The B-21 seeded-error benchmark in Evidence Annex I (16,000 trials, bootstrap confidence intervals) gives the R_max = 3 recursion bound conditional support: depth 3 pays only under hard-residue, low-correlation regimes, and depth 4 or more never pays in any tested cell. For the practitioner, this is the most directly actionable configuration result in the corpus — guidance with a confidence interval attached. The honest boundary travels with it: B-21 is a simulation against a synthetic adversary, and the result must not be quoted without its regime.
