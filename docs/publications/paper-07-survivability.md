## Abstract

The degradation lattice (cloud, local, edge, paper, human) as a chain of capability envelopes; descent and ascent predicates; the safety-equivalence invariant defining graceful degradation as losing capability without losing constitutional safety; deferred obligations and the recovery log.

## Merit

A governance framework that depends on connectivity is a governance framework for rich countries and good weather. This paper formalizes the alternative: the degradation lattice — cloud, local, edge, paper, human — as a chain of capability envelopes, with descent and ascent predicates governing movement between them, and the safety-equivalence invariant defining graceful degradation precisely: losing capability without losing constitutional safety.

For the engineer building for infrastructure-fragile environments, the merit is that offline operation is a designed mode with obligations attached — deferred obligations are logged, not dropped, and the recovery log governs the climb back up the lattice. For the policymaker and the global-health funder, the paper makes a formal argument that is usually only asserted: safety need not be a function of infrastructure. Descent all the way to paper and human operation is inside the model, not outside it.

**Who should read this work**

- Engineers deploying clinical or governance systems in low-connectivity or failure-prone infrastructure.
- Policymakers and funders evaluating systems for majority-world deployment.

**What it enables**

- A formal degradation lattice with descent/ascent predicates — offline, edge, and paper operation as designed modes.
- The safety-equivalence invariant: a checkable definition of graceful degradation.
- Deferred obligations and the recovery log: nothing is silently dropped when capability returns.
