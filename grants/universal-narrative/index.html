    Universal Grant Narrative Template¶

    Version: 1.0.0 Author: Dr. Christabel Odeta — SAVANT FRAMEWORK · CLAI-OS · AEGIS-GLOBAL Last Updated: 2026-08-11 SHA-256: [PENDING-FIRST-RELEASE]

    1. Purpose¶

    This template is the single narrative instrument through which the SAVANT FRAMEWORK seeks funding, regardless of funder. It is "universal" because its fourteen sections answer, in fixed order, every question a competent due-diligence process can ask — and because it refuses to vary its claims by audience. A narrative that changes its numbers per funder is not a narrative; it is a performance. The sections below carry per-section guidance, and §15 provides a worked skeleton. Any submitted narrative is a governed artifact (S50 class C); its figures must trace to the constitutional documentation (Manufacturing Specification, Node Types, Mesh Protocol, Compliance Adapters) or to signed field records.

    2. The Fourteen Sections¶

    Section 1 — Architectural Summary¶

    Guidance: One page. Name the stack (SPOS/SIP/S50 governance; CLAI-OS clinical engine; AEGIS-NG mesh; AEGIS-GLOBAL manufacturing and training) and state, in three sentences, what is built, what is governed, and what is transferable. Do not use adjectives that cannot be traced to a specification page. Every technical claim in this section must carry an internal citation to the canonical document that proves it.

    Section 2 — Falsifiability Evidence¶

    Guidance: The section most narratives omit, and the load-bearing section of this one. Enumerate the falsifiability tests from the canonical documents — group field tests (Node Types §7), link margin measurement (Mesh Protocol §4), manufacturing gate test (Manufacturing Specification §10) — and commit the program to executing them under funder-observable conditions. Offer the funder the standing right to run any test, unannounced. A program that cannot survive this section does not deserve funding; a program that can has no equal in due diligence.

    Section 3 — Field Deployment Metrics¶

    Guidance: Report measured numbers only, each tagged with provenance: node counts by type, uptime, message delivery rates, sync convergence times, tamper events and their handling. Where a metric is projected rather than measured, it is labeled PROJECTED and bound to a tranche gate (Section 13). No unlabeled projections. The distinction between measured and projected is the entire credibility of this section.

    Section 4 — Budget Architecture¶

    Guidance: Build the budget from the canonical unit economics: $46.30 per unit (Manufacturing Specification §2), $975 per certified engineer (Curriculum §2), logistics at DAP Lagos rates. State the efficiency dividend where relevant (e.g., $237,226 on a $2.5M program against Western-only sourcing). Every line must be traceable to a specification figure or a signed quote. Contingency is a named line with a named release rule, never a padding percentage dissolved across the budget.

    Section 5 — Compliance Alignment¶

    Guidance: Map the deployment jurisdiction to the P61–P70 adapter set (Compliance). Name the regulatory anchors (e.g., NDPR for Nigeria; GDPR/EU AI Act for EU-linked funders; DPDP for India-linked programs) and show the configuration-as-compliance mechanism. Disclose the planned-not-filed status of the PPA-006 Jurisdiction Compiler where jurisdictions exceed the adapter set. A funder must never learn of a compliance gap from anyone but this narrative.

    Section 6 — Sustainability Model¶

    Guidance: Show the post-grant operating state: training-only revenue to the architect ($975/engineer, no hardware margins — Curriculum §1), solar-only field power (no fuel chain), local QC and maintenance capacity, and the operator's ownership of all deployed capacity. The test the funder applies is: what breaks when the grant ends? The honest answer for this architecture is: nothing that was funded stops working, because the grant buys capacity, not subscriptions.

    Section 7 — Risk Register¶

    Guidance: Tabulate risks with owner, likelihood, impact, and mitigation — and include the risks other narratives omit: supply-chain concentration (mitigated by the canonical registry and local assembly capacity), key-person risk (mitigated by the documentation's transferability standard), currency exposure, and political transition risk. Every mitigation must be a mechanism, not an intention. "We will monitor closely" is not a mitigation and does not appear here.

    Section 8 — Partnership Contact¶

    Guidance: A single authoritative channel: grants@savantframework.com. State the response-time commitment and the identity of the constitutional authority (Dr. Christabel Odeta) for signature-level matters. No rotating contact lists; a funder should never have to guess which inbox is real.

    Section 9 — Downloadable PDF¶

    Guidance: The narrative must be exportable as a single self-contained PDF without layout degradation — this documentation site is built to that standard (tables and ASCII diagrams, no wide raster assets). Provide the PDF alongside the web form; never force a funder to choose between a rich submission and a printable one.

    Section 10 — Video Evidence¶

    Guidance: Link field footage with the same provenance discipline as metrics: location, date, and what claim the footage evidences. Video is corroboration, never a substitute for Section 3's measured metrics. If footage contradicts a metric, the footage wins and the metric is corrected — stated here so the rule precedes the event.

    Section 11 — Testimonial Architecture¶

    Guidance: Testimonials are structured evidence, not decoration. Each carries: named institution, role of the signatory, the specific capability attested, and a verification channel. Unsigned praise and unattributed quotes are excluded by policy. Where confidentiality binds a partner, use an anonymized institutional descriptor plus an offer of direct reference contact under NDA — never an unverifiable glowing quote.

    Section 12 — Timeline: 20-Week Pilot → 5-Year National Scale¶

    Guidance: Present the canonical arc:

    Phase Duration Content Exit Gate
    Pilot Weeks 1–20 One ward: node manufacturing/kitting (wks 1–6), engineer certification cohort (wks 4–8), deployment (wks 9–14), soak + falsifiability battery (wks 15–20) All group field tests pass under funder observation
    Regional Months 6–18 3–5 LGAs; hub densification; second and third cohorts 18-hour isolation test at LGA scale; QC transfer to local authority
    National Years 2–5 State-by-state scale-out; manufacturing ramp; compliance adapters per state/jurisdiction Sovereign operational independence per state

    Dates shift with funding timing; gates do not shift with dates. The exit gates are the contract, not the calendar.

    Section 13 — Metric-Gated Tranches¶

    Guidance: Request disbursement in tranches bound to measurable gates, and write the gates into the grant agreement. Canonical structure: Tranche 1 (pilot build, ~25 %), Tranche 2 (pilot gate pass, ~35 %), Tranche 3 (regional gates, ~25 %), Tranche 4 (national milestones, ~15 %). Percentages flex per funder; the principle does not: money follows measured performance, and a failed gate pauses disbursement rather than triggering narrative revision. Volunteering tranche gating is the strongest anti-corruption signal a proposal can send, which is why this template makes it non-optional.

    Section 14 — Anti-Corruption Enforcement¶

    Guidance: State the enforcement machinery plainly: metric-gated tranches (Section 13); procurement against the canonical registry with no broker substitution (Manufacturing Specification §5); hash-chained manifests from factory to field; the architect's training-only revenue (no hardware margin to inflate); QC gates that money cannot accelerate; and the funder's standing right to unannounced falsifiability testing (Section 2). Close with the commitment that any discovered irregularity is disclosed to the funder with a signed incident record — the same REGRESSION-record discipline the framework applies to itself.

    4. Standing Rules for Any Submission¶

    1. Every figure traces to a canonical document or a signed record. A narrative citing a number that exists nowhere else is a defect regardless of the number's accuracy.
    2. The fourteen sections appear in order. Funders may skim; they may not be denied the standard shape.
    3. The submission package (web + PDF + video links) is hashed and the hash retained; the narrative's own provenance follows the genealogy discipline of SPOS §6.

    Falsifiability Test¶

    Claim: This template produces narratives that are fully traceable, tranche-gated, and incapable of hiding a fabricated figure.

    Test 1 (traceability): Take any submitted narrative instance. Audit every quantitative claim against the canonical documents and signed field records. One untraceable figure falsifies §4 rule 1; the narrative fails its class-C checkpoint and is withdrawn.

    Test 2 (gate integrity): Inspect any funded program's disbursement history against its Section 13 gates. One tranche released against a failed or skipped gate falsifies the metric-gated tranche commitment and is a class-A defect against this template.

    Test 3 (uniformity): Compare two narratives submitted to two different funders for the same program. Any divergence in measured metrics (as opposed to formatting or emphasis) falsifies §1's universality claim.
